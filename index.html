Welcome & Family Acknowledgment

A Message from the Family

On behalf of the family of the late Nehemiah Akagburuonye Amuchie of Umuoshike, Ogbor Uvuru, we welcome you with grateful hearts. We give God the glory for one hundred and five remarkable years granted to our matriarch, Mrs. Nneoma Monica Nwafo Akagburuonye — affectionately known across Ogbor Uvuru and beyond as “Mama Nwa-afo.”

Her journey from a young girl in colonial Igboland to becoming matriarch, church founder and mother to generations is a testament to faith, resilience and love. From 2–8 November 2026, family, clergy and community gather across Ogbor Uvuru and Aboh Mbaise to honour her home-call. We invite you to walk with us through this page as we celebrate a life well lived, a faith well kept, and a legacy that remains.

Her Story

The Centenarian's Odyssey

Five chapters. One hundred and five years. A journey from birth to eternal legacy.

Chapter I • Birth & Girlhood Early 1920s

Dawn of a Matriarch

Colonial Igboland Youth & Ancestral Roots

Born in the early 1920s in Umuoshike, Ogbor Uvuru, Aboh Mbaise, into a family rooted in farming and tradition, the young Nneoma grew up amid the rhythms of colonial-era Igboland — the crow of the cockerel at dawn, the songs of the moonlight play, and the discipline of the family compound. She was raised in the fear of God and the wisdom of her elders, distinguished even as a girl for her diligence, her gentle wit, and an unshakable sense of duty to family and community that would define the century to come.

Chapter II • Marriage & Motherhood Begins Mid-Century

Matrimony & a Growing House

A Union with Nehemiah Akagburuonye Amuchie

In her youth, she was joined in marriage to Nehemiah Akagburuonye Amuchie of Umuoshike, Ogbor Uvuru, becoming the pillar of a growing household. Through years of both plenty and hardship, she provided for and raised her children with resourcefulness and unfailing faith, laying the foundation of a family that would grow to five generations — children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren and beyond.

Chapter III • Faith & Family A Life of Service

Mother of a Church, Mother of a Community

Founding Faith & a Household of Prayer

As her family grew, Nneoma became known throughout Ogbor Uvuru simply as “Nwa-afo” — a name of deep affection carried by generations who came to call her Mama. She is remembered within her community as the founding heart of what is today the Assemblies of God church at Ogbor Uvuru, now home to the Noah's Ark auditorium built in her honour by her family. Her life of prayer and quiet devotion became the spiritual root from which her children's own ministries and charitable works would later grow.

Chapter IV • Grandchildren & Golden Wisdom The Centenary Years

The Matriarchal Golden Era

A Compound of Blessing, A Memoir of Faith

Now a great-grandmother many times over, Nneoma's compound in Ogbor Uvuru became a place of pilgrimage for family seeking her blessing and counsel. Her later years were marked by an extraordinary vitality of spirit — celebrated at 104, then 105, with family gathered from across Nigeria and the diaspora. Her memoir, Me and My God, chronicling her walk of faith, was launched in her honour, and her matriarchal stature drew warm recognition and well-wishes from leaders across her state and nation.

Chapter V • Legacy & Eternal Rest 2026

A Glorious Exit at 105

Peaceful Home-Going & a Legacy That Remains

Her 104th and 105th birthdays were celebrated at the family's Ogbor Uvuru compound with the same joy and gratitude that marked her whole life — a testament to a life of faith rewarded. Surrounded by five generations of descendants, she quietly answered the call of her Creator in 2026, leaving behind a legacy of faith, family and community service that will echo through Umuoshike, Aboh Mbaise, and far beyond for generations to come.

Family Tributes

Voices of Those Who Loved Her

“

Tribute by Chief Barr. Engr. Dr. Success Obioma Akagburuonye

Mama, you were the roots that held our family tree steady through every storm. I watched you turn scarcity into sufficiency and fear into faith. You taught me that leadership is service, and that a name is only as strong as the character behind it. Everything HopeRising and Noah's Ark stand for today began at your feet. Rest well, our foundation — until we meet again.

“

Tribute by the Senior Daughter (Ada Nne / Nne Umuada)

As the Ada of this family, I stood closest to your wrapper as a girl and closest to your wisdom as a woman. You taught me the songs of the Umuada, the strength of a woman's word, and the grace of forgiveness. You were my first teacher of prayer and my last confidant in old age. Sleep on, Nne Nne — the daughters you raised will keep your fire burning.

“

Tribute by the Second Son & Family

Growing up in your household meant growing up disciplined, prayerful and proud of who we were. You never raised your voice, yet your word was law — spoken always with love. My own children called you 'Great Mama' with the same reverence I held as a boy. Thank you for one hundred and five years of unwavering example.

“

Tribute by the Second Daughter & Family

You were the calm in every storm our family faced. I remember your hands — always working, always giving, never idle even in your latest years. You showed us that faith and hard work are not opposites but companions on the same road. Go well, Mama.

“

Joint Tribute by the Younger Sons & Daughters

To the last-born children of this remarkable woman, you were never 'the last' in her love — she gave each of us her whole heart, every time. We remember evenings of storytelling, proverbs that still guide our decisions, and a home that always smelled of freshly pounded yam and prayer. Adieu.

“

Tribute by the Senior Son-in-Law

When I married into this family, I gained more than a wife — I gained a second mother in you. You welcomed me as Ogo with open arms and taught me the customs of Ogbor Uvuru with patience and warmth. Thank you for a home that always had room for one more.

“

Joint Statement by the In-Law Families (Ndi Ogo)

We, the in-law families united by marriage to this great house, join our voices in gratitude and farewell. Nneoma bridged families, settled disputes with gentle wisdom, and reminded every one of us that in-laws, too, are children of the same God.

“

Tribute by the Senior Grandson

Grandma, you were my first hero. I remember sitting at your feet, listening to stories of the old ways while you shelled melon seeds without ever looking down. I promise to carry the family name the way you carried this family.

“

Collective Tribute by the Granddaughters (Ndi Muo Nwanyi)

Grandma, you showed us what it means to be a woman of substance — graceful yet unbending, gentle yet formidable. We are the daughters of your daughters, carrying pieces of you into every room we enter. Ndewo nne, until we meet in glory.

“

Collective Tribute by the Grandsons (Ndi Nwa Nne)

You called us your little men, and you raised us to be exactly that — men of honour. We will tell our own children about the woman who lived through a century and never once lost her faith.

“

Tribute by the Diaspora Grandchildren (UK, USA, Canada)

Though oceans separated us from your compound, you were never far from our hearts. You taught us, even from a distance, that home is not a place but a person — and you were ours.

Young Voices

Innocent Reflections — The Youngest Generations

“Great-Grandma always had sweets in her wrapper pocket for us.”
“She called every one of us her 'own special one' — and we believed her.”
“Her stories about the olden days felt like magic.”
“She prayed for us by name, every single one, every single day.”
“Her laughter could fill the whole compound.”
“Even at 105, she remembered every one of our birthdays.”

To the youngest branches of this ancient tree — you may never remember her voice, but you will forever carry her blood, her name, and her blessing. Before she departed, she prayed over the fifth generation, that faith, unity and hard work would remain the inheritance of this family long after every name on this page has been forgotten by the world, but not by God.

May you grow to hear her story not as history, but as an instruction for how to live.
